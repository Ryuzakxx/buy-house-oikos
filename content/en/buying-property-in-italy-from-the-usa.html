Can Americans buy property in Italy?

Yes. Citizens of countries outside the EU can buy property in Italy when their country gives Italians the same right (the reciprocity condition, article 16 of the preliminary provisions to the Civil Code). For US citizens this is generally satisfied for buying a home; the notary checks it before the deed. If you hold an EU passport as well, you can buy as an EU citizen.

Staying in Italy

  • As a US citizen you can spend up to 90 days in any 180-day period in the Schengen area without a visa. Owning a home in Italy doesn't extend this.
  • To stay longer without working in Italy, the usual route is the elective residence visa, requested at the Italian consulate responsible for where you live in the US. It requires stable passive income and accommodation in Italy.
  • See relocation for the steps after you arrive.

Documents to prepare in the US

  • Codice fiscale: request it at your Italian consulate before you make an offer. See codice fiscale.
  • Power of attorney, if you won't attend the deed: signed before a US notary and apostilled by the Secretary of State of the state where it was notarised, or signed at an Italian consulate.
  • Proof of the origin of funds, for the bank's and the notary's anti-money-laundering checks.

Moving the money

The price is paid in euros, by wire transfer or banker's draft, and the deed records how. Converting a large amount of dollars is where many buyers lose the most without noticing: compare your bank's rate with a specialist currency provider, and consider fixing the rate once the preliminary contract sets the deed date. See currency exchange.

Reporting and taxes in the US

  • FBAR and FATCA. An Italian bank account counts as a foreign financial account. US persons generally have to report foreign accounts on the FBAR when their combined balance exceeds $10,000 during the year, and may have FATCA reporting on Form 8938.
  • Rental income. If you let the property, the income is taxed in Italy and must also be reported on your US return. The US-Italy income tax treaty and foreign tax credits are designed to avoid paying twice on the same income.
  • Selling later. A gain may be taxable in both countries, with credits for Italian tax paid.
  • Inheritance. Italian property is subject to Italian inheritance tax. A US-Italy treaty on estate and inheritance taxes, dating from 1955, helps avoid double taxation.

Speak to a US tax adviser with international experience before you buy, not after.

Mortgages

US lenders rarely lend against property abroad. Italian banks do lend to non-residents, usually with a larger deposit and more documents than for residents. See mortgages for foreign buyers.

Working across time zones

When you book a video call, the available times are shown in your own time zone. During the purchase, we agree on a regular update slot that suits both sides.

--- faq Q: Do I need to be resident in Italy to buy? A: No. You can buy as a non-resident; you'll need a codice fiscale, and usually an Italian bank account for paying bills and taxes. Q: Can I sign the deed from the US? A: You can't sign it remotely yourself, but someone can sign for you with a power of attorney made before a US notary and apostilled, or at an Italian consulate. Q: Can I buy through a US LLC? A: It's possible but has tax and practical consequences in both countries. Get advice from tax advisers in the US and in Italy before choosing the structure.
