Areas buyers ask about

Valle d'Itria

The inland plateau between Alberobello, Locorotondo, Cisternino, Martina Franca and Ceglie Messapica, where trulli are scattered through the countryside. Green, hilly by Puglia's standards, cooler in summer evenings, and about half an hour's drive from the coast. Expect small rooms in old trulli, and strict limits on what you can add to them.

Ostuni and the Brindisi coast

The white hill town of Ostuni looks over a plain of ancient olive trees running down to the Adriatic. This is masseria country: fortified farmhouses on several hectares, some restored, many waiting. Brindisi airport is close, which makes it practical for frequent trips.

Monopoli and Polignano a Mare

Coastal towns south of Bari with old centres on the sea, train connections to Bari and a busier year-round life. Apartments and townhouses in the old towns, villas and masserie in the countryside behind.

Salento

The southern tip, around Lecce, Otranto, Gallipoli, Nardò and Santa Maria di Leuca. Baroque Lecce is a lively city all year; the coast offers both seas within a short drive. Farther from the airports (Brindisi is the nearest), and the part of Puglia hit hardest by Xylella.

Gargano

The promontory in the north, mostly national park, with cliffs, forests and seaside towns such as Vieste and Peschici. More remote, with fewer international buyers and a shorter season.

Local rules worth knowing

{{legal-note}}
  • The regional landscape plan. Much of Puglia's countryside and coast is covered by the regional landscape plan (PPTR). Extensions, new outbuildings and pools often need a landscape authorisation as well as a building permit. Check before you rely on a project.
  • The coastal band. National law protects the land within 300 metres of the shoreline (article 142 of the Cultural Heritage and Landscape Code, Legislative Decree 42/2004), which limits building close to the sea.
  • Building in the countryside. In agricultural zones, how much you can build or extend depends on the size of the plot and on the municipality's plan. A large plot doesn't guarantee permission to add a house.
  • Agricultural pre-emption. When a masseria comes with farmland, tenant farmers or farming neighbours may have the right to buy on the same terms. The notary will need proof that the procedure was followed.
  • Water. Many country houses rely on a well, a cistern or a water tanker rather than the mains. Ask early, and check that a well is authorised.

Olive trees and Xylella

Since it was detected in Salento in 2013, the bacterium Xylella fastidiosa has killed a large share of the olive trees there and has spread further north. It shapes what you see in the landscape, what an olive grove is worth and what you may have to do with dead trees on your land. Regional rules on removing and replanting trees apply; ask what state the trees are in and what's been done before you value a property for its grove.

The 7% regime for pensioners

Puglia is one of the southern regions where people receiving a foreign pension, who move their tax residence to a municipality with no more than 20,000 inhabitants, can opt to pay a 7% substitute tax on their foreign income (article 24-ter of the income tax code, TUIR). The option covers the year of the move and the following nine. You must not have been tax resident in Italy in the previous five years, and other conditions apply. Check the population of the municipality and your eligibility with a tax adviser before choosing the house.

Getting there and living there

  • Airports. Bari for the northern half (Monopoli, Polignano, the western Valle d'Itria); Brindisi for Ostuni, the Brindisi coast and Salento.
  • Trains. Bari and Lecce are on the Adriatic railway line towards Bologna and Milan, with local lines into the Valle d'Itria.
  • Seasons. The coast is busy in July and August and much quieter in winter. Lecce, Bari, Martina Franca and the larger towns live all year.
  • Healthcare. Hospitals in the main towns; for country houses, check the drive to the nearest one.

--- faq Q: Can I add a pool to a trullo or a masseria? A: Often, but in many areas a pool needs a landscape authorisation as well as a building permit, and some municipalities restrict pools in rural zones. Check feasibility with a surveyor before you make an offer that depends on it. Q: Which airport is more convenient, Bari or Brindisi? A: Bari for the northern half of the region (Monopoli, Polignano, much of the Valle d'Itria), Brindisi for Ostuni, the Brindisi coast and Salento. Check which one has flights to where you live. Q: Is Puglia lively all year? A: The cities and larger towns are. Many coastal villages and holiday areas are very quiet from October to May, which matters if you plan to live there or let the house outside summer. Q: Should Xylella stop me buying land with olive trees? A: Not necessarily, but it changes what the grove is worth and what you may have to do with it. Ask about the state of the trees and any obligations before you value the land. Q: Can I build a new house on agricultural land I buy? A: Only if the municipal plan allows it for that plot, which often depends on its size and on whether you're a farmer. Check with a surveyor at the municipality before you buy land for that purpose.
