Cadastral compliance

Since July 2010 the deed must state that the cadastral data and floor plans match the actual state of the property (conformità catastale). If an extension or an internal change was never recorded, it has to be put right before the deed. It's a common cause of delays, and easy to spot early.

Country houses

  • Agricultural pre-emption. Tenant farmers or farming neighbours can have a right to buy agricultural land on the same terms (prelazione agraria). The notary needs proof that the procedure was followed or doesn't apply.
  • Water. Mains connection, a well with its authorisation, or a cistern?
  • Wastewater. Septic systems must comply with regional rules.
  • Access. Is the road public, private, or across someone else's land with an easement?

When the checks happen

Ideally before the offer, and at the latest before the preliminary contract. If something can only be checked later, the offer can make the purchase conditional on the result.

--- faq Q: Doesn't the notary check all of this? A: The notary checks title, registered charges and the statements the deed requires. The physical condition of the building and many planning details are outside the notary's role; that's what the technical checks are for. Q: Who pays for the checks? A: The buyer usually commissions and pays for their own technical survey. Fees depend on the property and the professional; ask for a written quote. Q: What if the checks find a problem? A: It depends on the problem. The seller can put it right before the deed, the price can be renegotiated, or you can withdraw if the offer was conditional.
