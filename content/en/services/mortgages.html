What to expect

  • A lower loan-to-value than residents usually get, so a larger deposit. [REAL DATA: current range offered to non-residents by the banks Oikos works with]
  • Proof of income and tax returns from your country, often translated.
  • An Italian bank account and a codice fiscale.
  • A valuation of the property by a surveyor appointed by the bank (perizia).
  • A separate mortgage deed, normally signed before the same notary on the day of the purchase.

Timing

Approval can take several weeks after the valuation. If you need a mortgage, the offer and the preliminary contract should leave enough time before the deed, and can make the purchase conditional on the loan being granted.

What we do

Mortgage advice comes from a bank or a registered credit broker. We introduce you to them, give them the property documents they ask for, and keep the bank's timetable in step with the notary's.

Tax on the mortgage

Bank mortgages longer than 18 months normally pay a single substitute tax in place of registration taxes on the loan (DPR 601/1973): 0.25% of the amount borrowed when the purchase qualifies for first-home benefits, 2% otherwise. The bank deducts it when the loan is paid out.

--- faq Q: Can I use a mortgage from my home country? A: Some buyers borrow at home against property they already own there and buy in Italy without an Italian loan. Whether that's possible and sensible depends on your bank and your tax position; ask them and a tax adviser. Q: Do Italian banks lend in other currencies?
