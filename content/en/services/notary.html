Notaio (notary)
A public official appointed by the State who drafts and authenticates the deed of sale, checks that the seller has the right to sell, registers the transfer and collects the purchase taxes. The notary is impartial between buyer and seller.

Who chooses the notary

By custom the buyer chooses the notary and pays the notary's fees. If you take a mortgage, the same notary normally also prepares the mortgage deed, signed on the same day.

What we do

  • Suggest notaries in the area and get a written estimate (preventivo) from the one you choose.
  • Send the notary the documents gathered during the checks, so problems surface weeks before the signing, not on the day.
  • Go through the draft deed with you, and with your lawyer if you have one.
  • Book an interpreter if you don't speak Italian: the law requires one at the signing, and the deed comes with a translation.
  • Organise the payments, by bank transfer or banker's draft, which the deed must record.
  • Prepare a power of attorney if you can't be there.

Signing without travelling

You can be represented at the deed by someone holding your power of attorney (procura). It can be signed at an Italian consulate, or before a notary in your country; in that case it needs an apostille (or legalisation, for countries outside the Hague Convention) and a translation if it isn't in Italian. It must reach the Italian notary before the deed date, so we start on it early.

What the notary collects

At the deed the notary collects the purchase taxes on behalf of the State, together with their own fee. The taxes depend on who is selling and on whether the home will be your main residence: see Italian property taxes. Ask for the written estimate well before the deed, with the fee and the taxes listed separately.

--- faq Q: Does the notary speak English? A: Some do. If you don't understand Italian, the law requires an interpreter at the signing anyway and the deed comes with a translation, so the notary's own languages matter less than it seems. Q: How much does the notary cost? A: The fee depends on the price, the complexity of the deed and whether there's also a mortgage deed. Ask for a written estimate that separates the notary's fee from the taxes they collect. Q: Can the notary hold the purchase price until the sale is registered? A: Yes. Either party can ask the notary to hold the price in a dedicated account until the sale is registered without problems. See escrow.
