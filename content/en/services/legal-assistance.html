Notary and lawyer: the difference

In Italy the deed of sale is signed before a notary, a public official who must be impartial between buyer and seller. The notary checks that the seller can sell, registers the transfer and collects the taxes. A lawyer advises only you: on the offer and the preliminary contract, on anything unusual about the property, and on what to do if something goes wrong.

When a lawyer is worth it

  • Before you sign the offer or the preliminary contract, which are binding and usually drafted by the seller's side or the agent.
  • When the property has irregularities: works without permits, boundary disputes, agricultural land with pre-emption rights.
  • When the seller is a company, the heirs of an estate, or someone in financial difficulty.
  • When you buy through a company, or with a partner of another nationality, and the ownership structure matters for tax or inheritance.

How we work with lawyers

We don't give legal advice ourselves. We introduce you to independent lawyers, brief them on the property and the documents we already hold, and keep their work in step with the notary's timetable. The engagement and the fees are agreed directly between you and the lawyer.

--- faq Q: Is a lawyer required to buy property in Italy? A: No. The notary is required; a lawyer is your choice. For a simple purchase, the notary and careful checks may be enough. When anything is unusual, a lawyer on your side is worth the cost. Q: Can the notary advise me? A: The notary must explain the deed and its consequences to both parties, but stays impartial. They won't negotiate for you or advise you against the seller.
