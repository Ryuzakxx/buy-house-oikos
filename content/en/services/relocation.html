EU and non-EU citizens

EU citizens can live in Italy and register as residents at the municipality (anagrafe) when they stay longer than three months, showing health cover and sufficient resources or work. Citizens of other countries need a visa for stays beyond 90 days in any 180-day period in the Schengen area, then a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno), requested within eight days of arrival.

The elective residence visa

Non-EU citizens who will live on their own income without working in Italy often apply for the elective residence visa (residenza elettiva) at an Italian consulate. Requirements include stable passive income and accommodation in Italy, which is one reason to plan the purchase and the visa together. Each consulate sets the documents and the income it expects.

After you arrive

  • Residence registration at the municipality.
  • Registration with the national health service (SSN) or private health cover.
  • Your driving licence: conversion rules depend on the country that issued it.
  • Schools: state, private and international.
  • Tax residence: moving changes where you pay tax. Italy has special regimes for some new residents, such as the 7% substitute tax on foreign income for pensioners who move to small municipalities in the South (article 24-ter of the income tax code, TUIR). The conditions are strict, so take advice before you move.

--- faq Q: Does buying a home give me the right to live in Italy? A: No. Owning property doesn't grant a visa or a residence permit. Non-EU buyers who want to live in Italy need a visa that fits their situation. Q: Do I become tax resident in Italy by buying a house? A: Not by buying alone. Tax residence depends on where you're registered, where you live for most of the year and where your main interests are. Get advice before you move.
