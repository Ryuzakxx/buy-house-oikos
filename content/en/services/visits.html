Video first, then in person

For a first look, a consultant can walk through a property with you on a video call: the rooms, the view, the road that leads to it, the sounds around it. It won't replace a visit, but it saves a flight for homes that would have been ruled out in the first five minutes.

Planning a viewing trip

When there are homes worth seeing in person, we group them by area and plan the route. We confirm appointments with owners and agents, check access (some country houses are reached by unpaved roads) and tell you which documents we've already seen for each property. You receive:

  • a schedule with addresses and driving times between viewings;
  • for each home, the price, how it compares with your brief and what still has to be checked;
  • where it helps, a second visit at a different time of day, to see the light or hear the traffic.

After each visit

We ask what you liked and what put you off, and record it in your brief. If you turn down a home because it's too far from the sea, distance to the sea becomes a stronger criterion in the next proposals.

What to bring

  • Your passport or ID card.
  • Your codice fiscale, if you have it: you'll need it to sign an offer.
  • Shoes for walking land and stairs.
--- faq Q: Can a consultant visit on my behalf? A: Yes. A consultant can visit alone and show you the property live on a video call, or send photos and notes on the points you care about. Q: How many viewings fit into a day? A: It depends on distances. In the countryside, three or four with travel time between them make a full day; in a town you can see more. Q: Can I go back to see a property again before making an offer? A: Yes, and for a property you're serious about we recommend it, ideally with a surveyor or builder if work is needed.
