How an offer works in Italy

A purchase offer (proposta d'acquisto) is normally written on a form, signed by you and often accompanied by a deposit, by cheque or transfer, held by the agent. Once the seller signs it and you are informed of the acceptance, it binds both sides. That's why we read it with you before you sign it, not after.

An offer usually states:

  • the price and how it will be paid;
  • the deposit that comes with the offer, and what happens to it if the seller says no;
  • the deadline for the seller's answer;
  • the dates for the preliminary contract and for the deed;
  • conditions, for example that the purchase depends on a mortgage being granted or on the outcome of the checks.

What we do

  • We compare the asking price with sales nearby and with the value ranges the Agenzia delle Entrate publishes for each area (Osservatorio del Mercato Immobiliare).
  • We use what the checks found, such as missing permits, necessary works or condominium arrears, as arguments in the negotiation.
  • We draft conditions that protect you, with a lawyer when the case calls for it.
  • We hold the conversation in Italian and report every exchange to you in writing.

Who is paid by whom

In the concierge service we work from your brief and on your side of the table. When a home comes from a partner agency, that agency shares part of its commission with us if you buy it. We tell you when this applies, so you always know who is paid by whom.

--- faq Q: Is a signed offer binding? A: Once the seller accepts it and you're informed of the acceptance, yes: you can't withdraw without consequences, which usually means losing the deposit. Read every clause before you sign; we go through it with you. Q: How much below the asking price should I offer? A: There's no standard discount. It depends on how long the home has been for sale, what the checks reveal and what sold nearby, which we look up case by case. Q: What is the caparra confirmatoria? A: A deposit, usually paid at the preliminary contract. If the buyer walks away, the seller keeps it; if the seller walks away, they must pay back twice the amount (article 1385 of the Italian Civil Code). The amount is agreed between the parties.
