When the money is due

  1. With the offer: a deposit, often by cheque or transfer held by the agent until the seller answers.
  2. At the preliminary contract: the main deposit (caparra confirmatoria), and sometimes an advance on the price. The offer deposit usually becomes part of it.
  3. At the deed: the balance of the price, the taxes and the notary's fee. If you have a mortgage, the bank pays its share at the same signing.

Each payment must be traceable, by bank transfer or banker's draft, and the deed states how the price was paid.

Cash or mortgage

  • Cash is simpler and faster, and makes your offer stronger: no condition on a loan, no bank valuation.
  • An Italian mortgage is possible for non-residents, usually with a larger deposit than residents and several weeks for approval. See mortgages for foreign buyers.
  • Borrowing at home, for example against property you already own there, can be cheaper or simpler. Check the tax and currency effects with an adviser.

If your money isn't in euros

The exchange rate between the preliminary contract and the deed can move the cost by more than any negotiation. Compare your bank with specialist currency providers, and consider fixing the rate once the deed date is set. See currency exchange.

Where the money comes from

Banks, notaries and agents must check the origin of the funds under anti-money-laundering rules. Have documents ready: a sale of property, savings statements, an inheritance, a loan agreement.
