Notaio
A legal professional appointed by the State as a public official. The notary authenticates deeds, which gives them full legal proof, and is responsible for registering property transfers. In a sale, the notary is impartial between buyer and seller.

What the notary does

  • Checks the identity and capacity of the parties.
  • Checks the seller's title and the mortgages, seizures and other charges registered against the property, normally over the previous twenty years.
  • Collects the statements required by law: cadastral compliance, planning information, energy certificate, payment methods, the agent's details and commission.
  • Drafts the deed, reads it to the parties and explains it.
  • Collects the purchase taxes and pays them to the State.
  • Registers the deed and records the transfer in the property registers and the cadastre.

What the notary doesn't do

  • Negotiate for you, or advise you against the seller.
  • Inspect the physical condition of the building.
  • Check every planning detail on site: the notary relies on documents and declarations, so a technical survey is still worth having.

Choosing and paying the notary

By custom the buyer chooses the notary and pays the fee. There's no fixed national tariff, so ask for a written estimate (preventivo) that separates the notary's fee from the taxes they collect for the State. If you take a mortgage, the mortgage deed is usually signed before the same notary, the same day.

The day of the deed

  1. Everyone meets at the notary's office: buyer and seller (or their attorneys), the agent if there is one, the interpreter if needed.
  2. The notary checks identities and reads the deed. With an interpreter, it's translated for you, and a written translation is attached to the deed.
  3. Any last corrections are made, then everyone signs.
  4. The balance is paid, usually by banker's drafts or a transfer confirmed in advance, and the deed records how.
  5. You receive the keys.

Bring: your passport or ID card, your codice fiscale, the means of payment, and, if you're married, information about your matrimonial property regime, which affects who owns the house.

Signing through a power of attorney

If you can't be there, someone can sign for you with a power of attorney (procura). It can be made at an Italian consulate, or before a notary in your country; then it needs an apostille (for Hague Convention countries) or legalisation, and a sworn translation if it isn't in Italian. Agree its wording with the Italian notary first, so it covers everything the deed needs.

After the deed

The notary registers the deed with the Agenzia delle Entrate and records the transfer in the property registers, then the cadastre is updated in your name. You can ask for a certified copy of the deed once registration is complete.
