Who can borrow

Italian banks lend to non-residents, including non-EU citizens, though not every bank does and conditions vary. Expect the bank to look at your income, your existing debts in your own country and your credit history there, which it can't easily verify, so it will ask for more documents than from a resident.

Documents usually requested

  • Passport and codice fiscale.
  • Proof of income: payslips, tax returns, or company accounts if you're self-employed, often with a translation.
  • Bank statements for recent months.
  • Details of existing loans and commitments.
  • The property documents: the preliminary contract, cadastral data, planning information.
  • An Italian bank account, usually with the lending bank.

The steps

  1. Pre-assessment: a bank or broker tells you roughly what you could borrow, before you make an offer.
  2. Application: once you have a property, ideally with the offer conditional on the loan.
  3. Valuation (perizia): a surveyor appointed by the bank values the property. If the valuation is lower than the price, the loan may be reduced.
  4. Approval and the bank's offer, with the final conditions.
  5. Mortgage deed: signed before the notary, usually on the same day as the purchase, when the bank pays the money.

Fixed or variable

A fixed rate gives certainty over the whole term; a variable rate follows a market index and can rise or fall. If your income is in another currency, you'll carry exchange-rate risk as well as rate risk; a fixed rate removes one of the two.

Costs

  • Application fee and valuation fee, set by the bank.
  • Substitute tax: 0.25% of the loan if the purchase qualifies for first-home benefits, 2% otherwise (for loans over 18 months).
  • Notary's fee for the mortgage deed.
  • Building insurance against fire and other damage, which banks require on the mortgaged property.
  • Any life or payment-protection insurance the bank proposes is optional unless the contract says otherwise; compare it with alternatives.

Timing

From application to approval, allow several weeks; more if documents need translating or certifying. Make sure the preliminary contract leaves enough time before the deed, and that the offer is conditional on the mortgage being granted, so you don't lose the deposit if the bank says no.
