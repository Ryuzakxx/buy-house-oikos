Title and the chain of ownership

The notary checks the entries in the property registers, normally over the last twenty years. Pay attention when the seller inherited the property: if the inheritance was never formally accepted, the notary will ask for it to be put right before the deed, and it takes time. Properties owned by several heirs need all of them, or their attorneys, to sign.

Charges and claims

A mortgage registered by the seller's bank is common and isn't a problem, as long as it's paid off at the deed and then cancelled. A seizure (pignoramento) or a legal claim recorded against the property is a different matter: don't sign anything until a lawyer has explained the consequences. The notary's dedicated account can protect the payment of the price in these cases.

Cadastral and planning compliance

  • Cadastral compliance. Since July 2010 the deed must state that the cadastral data and plans match the actual property. Unrecorded changes must be updated by a surveyor before the deed.
  • Planning compliance. A difference between the building and its permits (abuso edilizio) is more serious: some can be regularised by applying for a retrospective permit and paying a fine, others can't. Know which before you commit.
  • Amnesties (condoni). Some buildings were legalised under past amnesty laws. Check that the amnesty was actually granted, not just applied for.

Constraints on the property

  • Landscape protection. Coastal bands, lakeshores, woods and many rural areas are protected; works need a landscape authorisation.
  • Heritage listing. For listed buildings, the State has a right of pre-emption on the sale, and works need approval from the heritage office (Soprintendenza).
  • Agricultural pre-emption. Tenant farmers and some farming neighbours can have the right to buy agricultural land on the same terms.
  • Civic uses (usi civici). Some land, especially in the South, is subject to historic collective rights that limit what can be sold or built.
  • Easements. Rights of way, water pipes, power lines crossing the land.

Apartments: the condominium

Ask the building manager (amministratore) for a statement of the seller's position, the budget, the minutes of recent meetings and the works approved. The buyer can be held liable, together with the seller, for unpaid charges of the current year and the previous one, and extraordinary works already approved can be charged to whoever owns the flat when they're paid.

Warning signs

  • The seller can't produce the planning file, or says "it's always been like that".
  • The price is far below similar properties without a clear reason.
  • The seller wants part of the price unofficially, outside the deed.
  • Rooms or buildings exist that don't appear in the cadastral plan.
  • Pressure to sign the offer before you've seen the documents.
--- faq Q: When should the checks be done? A: As early as possible: ideally before the offer, at the latest before the preliminary contract. If a check can only be completed later, make the offer conditional on its result. Q: Who orders the checks, the agent or me? A: The seller should provide the basic documents. Independent checks on your side, such as a technical survey or a lawyer's review, are ordered and paid by you. Q: Can a planning irregularity be fixed after I buy? A: Sometimes, but the deed may be blocked by it, the price should reflect it, and some irregularities can't be legalised at all. Get a professional opinion before you commit.
