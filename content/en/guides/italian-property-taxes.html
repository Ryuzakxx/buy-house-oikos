Taxes when you buy

Which taxes you pay depends on two things: who is selling (a private person, or a company charging VAT) and whether the home will be your main residence and qualify for first-home benefits (prima casa).

Purchase taxes on a home
SellerMain home (prima casa)Other homes
Private individual, or a company selling without VATRegistration tax 2% (minimum €1,000), mortgage tax €50, cadastral tax €50Registration tax 9% (minimum €1,000), mortgage tax €50, cadastral tax €50
Builder or company selling with VATVAT 4%, plus registration, mortgage and cadastral tax of €200 eachVAT 10% (22% for luxury categories A/1, A/8, A/9), plus €200 each

A builder or renovation company selling within five years of completing the work charges VAT; after five years it can choose. The notary will tell you which column applies.

The cadastral value (prezzo-valore)

When an individual buys a home from a private seller, the registration tax is normally calculated on the cadastral value, not on the price, provided the real price is stated in the deed. The cadastral value is the rendita catastale (from the land registry) multiplied:

  • rendita × 1.05 × 110 for a home that qualifies as prima casa;
  • rendita × 1.05 × 120 for other homes.

For most homes the cadastral value is well below the market price, which is why the purchase tax on a resale is often lower than buyers expect.

Who qualifies as prima casa

Generally: the home is not in luxury categories A/1, A/8 or A/9; you live in the municipality, or move your residence there within 18 months, or work there; and you don't already own a home in that municipality or, anywhere in Italy, a home bought with the same benefits. Most foreign buyers of a holiday home pay the "other homes" rates. The details and exceptions are for the notary to confirm.

Every year: IMU and TARI

IMU, the municipal property tax

IMU is not due on your main residence (unless it's in category A/1, A/8 or A/9). It is due on second homes, including holiday homes owned by people who live abroad.

  • Taxable base: rendita × 1.05 × 160 for homes.
  • Rate: set by each municipality. The standard rate is 0.86%; municipalities can raise it up to 1.06% (1.14% in some) or lower it.
  • Deadlines: two instalments, due on 16 June and 16 December.

Illustrative calculation: rendita €1,200 × 1.05 × 160 = €201,600. At 1.06%, IMU is €2,136.96 a year; at 0.86%, €1,733.76.

TARI, the waste tax

Set by the municipality based on the floor area and the use of the property. You register for it after the purchase.

If you let the property

  • Long-term lets: rental income is taxed under Italian income tax (IRPEF), or you can opt for a flat tax (cedolare secca) of 21%, or 10% for certain regulated contracts.
  • Short-term lets (under 30 days): the cedolare secca is 21% for one property chosen by the owner and 26% for the others let short-term.
  • If you don't let it, IMU generally replaces Italian income tax on the property.

Your country of residence may also tax the income, with relief under its tax treaty with Italy.

When you sell

A gain on a home sold within five years of purchase can be taxed in Italy, unless it was your main residence for most of that time. You can usually ask the notary to apply a substitute tax at the deed instead of declaring the gain. Ask your adviser about the rate and the rules in force when you sell.

--- faq Q: Is the purchase tax calculated on the price I pay? A: When an individual buys a home from a private seller, it's normally calculated on the cadastral value, which is usually lower than the price. When the seller charges VAT, VAT is calculated on the price. Q: Can I get first-home benefits if I don't live in Italy? A: Generally only if you move your residence to the municipality within 18 months or work there. Italian citizens registered abroad (AIRE) have specific rules. Ask the notary before the preliminary contract. Q: Do I pay IMU on a holiday home? A: Yes. IMU is due on homes that are not your main residence, at the rate set by the municipality, in two instalments a year. Q: Do non-residents pay Italian income tax on a property they don't let? A: Generally no: for a property that isn't let, IMU replaces Italian income tax on it. If you let it, the rental income is taxable in Italy.
