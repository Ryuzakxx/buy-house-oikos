1. We ask before we show

Most property searches start with listings that you then narrow down. We work the other way round: before we propose anything, we ask the eighteen questions behind a brief. Where, how much, what kind of house, what is around it and what you plan to do with it. You can answer in three ways:

  • The guided chat: typed, at your own pace, saved as you go.
  • The voice interview: the same questions read aloud; you answer by speaking and correct the transcript.
  • A video call with a consultant, who drafts the brief from the conversation. It is recorded and transcribed only if you agree.

2. Every answer gets a weight

After each answer you tell us how much it matters. Most searches skip this step, and it is the one that saves the most time.

Must have
Matters a lot. A home that misses it drops well down the list. For example: three bedrooms.
Nice to have
A bonus. It moves a home up the list but never rules one out. For example: a sea view.
Deal breaker
Without it, it's a no. A home that fails a deal breaker isn't proposed, however good the rest is. For example: ready to move in, no restoration project.

When something doesn't matter to you, say so: that answer is left out of the comparison, so it never hides a good house.

3. You read the brief and correct it

Your answers become a written brief in three columns. Before you send it, you can move any criterion from one column to another, remove it, or go back and change the answer. Nothing leaves your device until you send it. A consultant then reads it, may call you to go through it, and confirms it with you.

4. We search against it

Every home we know of is compared with the confirmed brief: Oikos listings, partner agencies' listings and what colleagues know before it is advertised. Deal breakers rule homes out, must haves count three times as much as nice to haves, and a must have missed outright pushes a home far down the list. Each home we propose comes with the criteria it meets, the ones it misses and the ones we still have to check on site.

If nothing fits yet, the search doesn't stop: we'll search through partner agencies, colleagues and other sources for you.

5. The brief learns from your feedback

After each proposal or visit we ask what worked and what didn't. If you turn a home down because it is too far from the sea, distance to the sea becomes a must have with a tighter limit. After a few rounds the brief no longer describes the house you imagined, but the one you will buy.

6. Then we guide the purchase

From the offer to the notary's deed and after it: checks, contracts, translations, money transfers and the work after the keys. See what we take care of.
