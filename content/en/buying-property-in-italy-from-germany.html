Buying as an EU citizen

German citizens can buy property in Italy without restrictions, and live there: if you stay longer than three months, you register as a resident at the municipality. Payments are in euros by SEPA transfer, so there's no currency risk.

What will look familiar, and what won't

Germany and Italy compared
TopicIn GermanyIn Italy
The notaryNotarielle Beurkundung of the sale contractThe deed (rogito) is signed before a notary, who is impartial and registers the transfer
Before the deedUsually no binding step before the notarised contractA signed offer becomes binding when accepted, then a preliminary contract (compromesso) with a deposit
Protecting the buyerPriority notice (Auflassungsvormerkung) in the GrundbuchA preliminary contract signed before a notary can be registered in the property registers (article 2645-bis Civil Code) for similar protection
RegistersGrundbuchProperty registers (Conservatoria) for ownership and charges, and the cadastre (Catasto) for the description and tax value
Purchase taxGrunderwerbsteuer on the priceRegistration tax, often calculated on the cadastral value rather than the price, or VAT when buying from a builder

The biggest difference in practice: in Italy you commit before you see the notary. Read the offer and the preliminary contract as carefully as you would read a German Kaufvertrag. See the buying process, step by step.

Tax

  • Rental income from the Italian property is taxed in Italy. Under the Germany-Italy double tax treaty, Germany generally exempts it but takes it into account for your income tax rate (Progressionsvorbehalt). Confirm with your Steuerberater.
  • Inheritance: Germany and Italy have no treaty on inheritance tax. Italian inheritance tax applies to the Italian property, and German inheritance tax can apply as well, with a credit for the Italian tax under German law. Plan ownership with this in mind, especially for larger values.

--- faq Q: Is the Italian notary the same as a German Notar? A: The role is similar: a public official, impartial, who authenticates the deed and registers the transfer. The difference is timing: in Italy the binding commitment usually happens earlier, with the accepted offer and the preliminary contract. Q: Do I need an Italian lawyer if there's a notary? A: Not necessarily. Many German buyers find the notary system familiar, but the offer and the preliminary contract are signed before the notary is involved, and that's where a lawyer on your side is most useful.
