Can British citizens buy property in Italy?

Yes. As non-EU citizens, British buyers are subject to the reciprocity condition, which is generally satisfied for buying a home; the notary checks it before the deed.

Staying in Italy after Brexit

  • You can spend up to 90 days in any 180-day period in the Schengen area without a visa. Owning a home doesn't extend this, which matters if you planned long summers.
  • For longer stays without working in Italy, the usual route is the elective residence visa, requested at the Italian consulate in the UK.
  • British citizens who were resident in Italy before the end of 2020 have rights under the Withdrawal Agreement; that's a separate situation.

Documents to prepare in the UK

  • Codice fiscale, from the Italian consulate in the UK or from an Agenzia delle Entrate office in Italy.
  • Power of attorney, if you won't be at the deed: signed before a UK notary public and apostilled by the FCDO Legalisation Office, or signed at the Italian consulate.
  • Proof of the origin of funds for anti-money-laundering checks.

Moving pounds into euros

Compare your bank's rate with a specialist currency provider, and consider a forward contract once the preliminary contract fixes the deed date. See currency exchange.

Tax in both countries

  • Rental income from the Italian property is taxed in Italy and must be declared to HMRC; the UK-Italy double taxation convention and foreign tax credit relief prevent paying twice on the same income.
  • Selling may create a gain taxable in both countries, with relief for Italian tax.
  • Inheritance: Italian property is subject to Italian inheritance tax, and UK inheritance tax may also apply depending on your situation. Take advice on both.

Mortgages

UK lenders rarely lend on Italian property. Italian banks lend to non-residents with a larger deposit than for residents. Some buyers instead release equity from a UK home. See mortgages for foreign buyers.

--- faq Q: Did Brexit make buying property in Italy harder? A: Buying itself is much the same. What changed is staying: the 90-in-180-days limit now applies, and long stays need a visa. Q: Do I need an Italian bank account? A: It's not required for the purchase, but it makes paying utilities, IMU and other local costs much simpler.
